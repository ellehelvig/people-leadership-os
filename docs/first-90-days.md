# First 90 days as Chief of Staff to the CPO

A plan for the first 90 days in the role at Harbor, the fictional company in this repository. It shows the order I would do things in and why. It is a plan, not a record of results.

The job, as I see it: give the CPO more time for the decisions only the CPO can make, and give everyone else a clear answer to three questions. What did we decide? Who owns the next step? When do we check?

## Five questions I would ask the CPO in week one

1. Which decisions do you want to make yourself, and which should never reach you?
2. Where does work stall today: no owner, no decision, or no follow-through?
3. Which relationships with Meridian Group need the most care this year?
4. What would make the weekly leadership meeting worth your time?
5. What should I never do on your behalf?

The answers set my boundaries. I would write them down, confirm them with the CPO, and share them with the People Leadership Team so nobody wonders what the Chief of Staff is for.

## Days 1 to 30: listen and map

| What I would do | What the CPO gets |
|---|---|
| Meet each People function lead and the CPO's key partners at Harbor and Meridian Group. Ask what they need from the CPO and what slows them down | A one-page summary of themes, with no attribution to individuals |
| List every open decision, commitment, and recurring meeting the CPO owns | One list instead of several inboxes, with a proposed owner for each item |
| Watch the current leadership meetings without changing them | A short note on what each meeting produces today and what it should produce |
| Find the decisions that have no owner | The [owner-gap note](../briefs/2026-w46-cpo-brief.md) in the weekly brief, started in week two |

**What I would not do yet:** change the meeting rhythm, add tools, or speak for the CPO in rooms I have not been asked into.

**How we would know it is working:** the CPO can name the three decisions that matter most this quarter, and every open item on the list has a proposed owner.

## Days 31 to 60: run the rhythm

| What I would do | What the CPO gets |
|---|---|
| Move leadership asks into [issues](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/) with two forms: decision requests and signals | One intake, and a record of every ask and answer |
| Start the [weekly brief](../briefs/2026-w46-cpo-brief.md): Copilot drafts from open issues, I edit, and the CPO reads one page | Decisions first, noise labeled as noise, and every claim traceable to its source |
| Set the [operating rhythm](operating-rhythm.md): weekly decisions, monthly health and alignment, quarterly direction | Meetings that end with a decision, an owner, and a date |
| Write the first [decision record](../decisions/0001-ai-enabled-people-operating-model.md) for a choice that is material or hard to reverse | Rationale that outlasts the meeting and the people in it |
| Agree how the team decides whether to adopt a parent program, using [align-or-diverge](align-or-diverge.md) | The same five tests every time, instead of a fresh debate |

**How we would know it is working:** asks arrive through issues instead of side messages, the brief goes out on the same day each week, and each meeting's decisions show up in an issue or record within a day.

## Days 61 to 90: make it the team's system

| What I would do | What the CPO gets |
|---|---|
| Hold a 30-minute retro with function leads on the rhythm: what to keep, change, or stop | A rhythm the team owns, not one imposed on it |
| Hand issue triage to function leads for their own areas | Less routing through the Chief of Staff, and faster answers |
| Bring the CPO a short list of work to stop, with the cost of continuing each item | Capacity back for the priorities that matter |
| Turn on an automated check so every brief is tested against the live issues | Confidence that the brief is current without rereading every issue |
| Agree with the CPO how my role should change for the next two quarters | A role that grows with the team instead of turning into a gatekeeper |

**How we would know it is working:** function leads triage their own issues, the CPO spends meeting time on decisions rather than updates, and the stop list frees time someone can name.

## Principles I would hold to

- **The CPO decides. I make the decision easy to make and hard to lose.**
- **Every claim cites its source.** If the brief says something, a reader can check it in one click.
- **AI drafts, a person decides.** Copilot writes the first version of the brief. I edit it, and the CPO acts on it. No employee data goes into a general AI tool.
- **Fewer meetings, better records.** A meeting is done when the issue or record says what was decided, who owns the next step, and when we check.
- **Make myself less necessary.** By day 90, the system should run without me in the middle of every ask.
